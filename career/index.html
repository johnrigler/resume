Why I relocated and worked locally

After a long career in enterprise technology, I relocated to western New Mexico expecting that my next long-term technical role would be remote. Before returning to a home office, I wanted to participate in the community where I was settling. I joined Acoma Business Enterprises in a customer-facing role and developed connections in Grants, the Acoma Pueblo, and to a lesser extent Albuquerque.

That relocation followed a finite period of independent exploration in emerging distributed-ledger technology, overlapping with time spent in Austin and other locations. The work produced software and technical ideas I can present to colleagues, including at professional gatherings in Denver. Having brought that exploratory phase toward a close, I am now looking to apply the combined experience in a stable remote enterprise technology role.

My career crosses telephone-based enterprise technical support, Unix/Linux engineering, production operations, cloud infrastructure, and a completed phase of independent technical research and development. I'm looking for remote roles that use both the technical depth and the experience explaining complex systems to people.

Why the process is public

After years in enterprise infrastructure, a geographically mobile period of independent technology exploration, and a subsequent move to western New Mexico, I wanted a structured way to return to remote technical employment. Instead of treating each posting as an isolated form, I built a small system for assessing roles, selecting truthful career evidence, and tracking next actions.

Design principles

  • Static HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript; no Node or backend required.
  • Public case study and experience summaries are deliberately separate from private job records.
  • Browser-only pipeline with explicit JSON export/import; no telemetry or accounts.
  • Readable source, portable files, and no vendor-specific build system.
  • GitHub Issues can track public development tasks, not private recruiting conversations.

This project is itself a demonstration of applied troubleshooting, information architecture, and lightweight software design. Inspect the source and workflow.
